Play on · iPhone & iPad

Play your PC games on iPhone and iPad — in Safari

Your gaming PC streams straight to the browser of your iPhone or iPad. Nothing from the App Store, no account — just a URL. MoonlightWeb turns it into a full game-streaming client.

What you get

  • A touch trackpad. One finger aims, two fingers scroll and pinch, taps click.
  • A real keyboard. The native virtual keyboard, with the keys a PC needs — Win, Esc, Ctrl, arrows.
  • Game controllers. Xbox and PlayStation controllers paired over Bluetooth work in the browser. No controller at hand? An on-screen one is built in.
  • Low latency. On your home Wi-Fi the delay stays under 20 ms — fast enough for action games, not just turn-based ones.
  • Any browser. Safari is enough; Chrome or Edge on iOS work the same way.

Set it up in three steps

Install MoonlightWeb on the gaming PC

Windows, macOS (Apple Silicon) or Linux — follow the install guide for your system. That PC is the only machine with something to install.

Open it in Safari

At home, open the PC's address on your network. Away from home, turn on Internet access on the PC and open the link it gives you. Your PC is already listed: type the PIN it shows, once — the iPhone or iPad is now trusted for good.

Turn it sideways and play

Pick a game or the whole desktop, hold the phone in landscape, connect a controller if you have one.

Playing on 4G, 5G or hotel Wi-Fi

Lower the streamed resolution and turn on Video Enhancement: the iPhone or iPad upscales and sharpens the picture on its own GPU, so a 720p stream looks close to 1440p while using a fraction of the data. If a network blocks UDP, the stream falls back to TCP on its own.

No cloud in the middle. The stream is a direct, peer-to-peer WebRTC connection between your device and your PC — no account, no subscription, and your games never pass through anyone else's servers.
